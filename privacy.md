# Privacy policy

**Dualtop** (the iPhone and iPad app) and **Dualtop for Windows** (the program for your PC)
Effective October 29, 2026 · updated October 30, 2026 (files and clipboard history)

The short version: Dualtop collects nothing about you. There's no account, no analytics, no ads and no tracking, and there are no Dualtop servers. What you make and what you connect to stays between your own devices.

## What stays on your phone

- **The desktop's documents and settings:** notes, files, saved windows, your choices in Settings. They're kept in the app's own storage on the phone, and the Files app can see them.
- **The computers you add in Remote:** their names, addresses and user names. Their passwords are kept in the phone's keychain, only on that phone.
- **Pairing with Dualtop for Windows:** the key that lets your phone and PC recognise each other is in the phone's keychain, and on the PC it's sealed to that PC.
- **Diagnostics** (Settings > Diagnostics): a short record of connection steps, to help when something doesn't work. It never holds passwords, keys or account names. It stays on the phone, and only goes anywhere if you copy it and send it yourself.

## What connects to what

- **Your computers.** Remote Desktop, VNC and Dualtop for Windows connect straight from your phone to the computers you choose, on your network or over your own VPN. Nothing passes through anyone else's server.
- **Email.** The desktop's Mail writes the message, and the phone's own Mail sends it; you press Send there.
- **The browser.** The pages you visit, and your chosen search engine, see your visits as they would in any browser.
- **Finding your computers.** The app looks for Dualtop for Windows and Macs sharing their screens on your local network (Bonjour). iOS asks your permission first.
- **Updates on the PC.** Dualtop for Windows asks GitHub whether a newer version exists. GitHub sees that request, as with any website. Nothing about you or your PC is sent.

## Permissions, and what each is used for

Each is asked for only when you first use what needs it, and each can be turned off in the phone's Settings.

- **Camera:** scans the pairing code on your PC, and documents you choose to scan into Files.
- **Microphone and speech recognition:** voice typing, only while you use it. Recognition happens on the phone where iOS supports that; otherwise Apple's speech service handles it, under Apple's privacy policy.
- **Photos:** only the pictures you pick.
- **Contacts:** Mail's contact picker gives Dualtop only the address you pick, without asking for access to your contacts.
- **Calendar and Reminders:** shown and changed on the phone, only after you allow it.
- **Local network:** connecting to your computers and finding them.

## The clipboard

With "Share the clipboard" on (Settings > Remote), text you copy on a connected computer is put on the phone's clipboard. "Paste from this phone" sends the phone's text to that computer. Turn it off and nothing moves between them.

## Files

Files move between the phone and a computer only in the ways you choose:

- **Over Remote Desktop,** only if you turn it on (Settings > Remote; it starts off). The PC you're connected to can then open one folder on the phone, Dualtop's own Files, and nothing else on the phone. It can only read it, or read and write too, as you chose, and only while you're connected.
- **With Dualtop for Windows,** a file goes to your PC only when you send it, and the PC asks "Save it?" before anything is written. Files the PC sends you land in the Downloads folder of Dualtop's Files.

## Clipboard history

The Clipboard app (Win+V) lists the last things you copied. It's kept only in the phone's memory while Dualtop is open, and is gone when it quits. Anything a password manager marks as concealed is left out.

## Children

Dualtop isn't directed at children and doesn't knowingly collect anything from anyone.

## Crash reports

If you've chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass on crash reports, which contain no personal content. That's Apple's setting, and it's yours to change.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
